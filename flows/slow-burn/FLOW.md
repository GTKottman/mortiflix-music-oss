# Slow burn

One long rise. The piece never drops out to build back up: every section keeps what the last one had and adds to it.
The listener should feel pulled, not pushed, and only notice how far they've come at the end.

## How it moves

- **The floor first.** Start with the pedal (a held or repeated bass note) and one ostinato. Nothing else needs to
  happen for a while: the patience is the point.
- **Add, never swap.** Each section brings one new thing: a part, a doubling, a higher register, more velocity. Keep
  everything already playing (a part can thin its rhythm, never leave).
- **The crescendo is real.** Velocity climbs across sections, not just the part count. Plan where it rises fastest.
- **The pedal moves late.** Hold the harmony over the pedal for most of the piece; let the bass finally move in the
  last third. That release is the piece's payoff.
- **The peak is late** (the last 40%), and the ending stays near it.

## The motif

A fragment (2-3 notes) in the first sections, answered by nothing. The complete motif arrives only at the peak.

## Traps

- A "breakdown" by habit before the last section: this flow forbids it. Keep the momentum.
- Rising only by adding drums. Rise in register and harmony too.
- An ending that fades back to the opening: end high.
